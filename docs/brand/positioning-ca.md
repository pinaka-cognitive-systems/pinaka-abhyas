# Positioning — CA Foundation (pinaka-abhyas)

The exam-specific layer on top of `brand-core.md`. The voice and the seven principles are inherited unchanged. What changes is who we speak to, what we call things, and what we lead with. The LSAT positioning does not transfer; only the doctrine does.

## The product, in one line

A free, open-source, offline desktop app where a CA Foundation aspirant practises verified questions and learns what they do not know, per topic and per misconception.

## Who it is for

A self-studying CA Foundation aspirant in India. Often a commerce student, late teens to early twenties, preparing alongside coaching or alone. Time-pressed. Anxious about a large syllabus. Wants to know, honestly, where they stand and what to do next. Treat them as capable. They are sitting one of the harder entry exams in the country.

## What we lead with

Free for students. Open-source app. Works offline. Your data stays on your device, and sharing is opt-in and anonymous. This is not a feature list bolted on; it is the position. We are not selling anything. We are trying to add value to students who are often sold to.

## Vocabulary

Use: topic, subtopic, misconception, problem, practice, mock, drill, skill, mastery, readiness, diagnosis, review.

Do not use: anything LSAT-specific (LR, RC, the 120 to 180 scale). Any pricing language, because the app is free. "Predicted score" is banned until we can back it with data; say "readiness estimate" and label it honestly as an estimate.

## What readiness means here

The Paper 3 pass bar is 40 out of 100, net of negative marking (plus one per correct, minus a quarter per wrong). Readiness is shown as honest distance to that bar, as an estimate, never as a promise. We do not invent a predicted mark we cannot stand behind.

## Tone check for any CA surface

State the diagnosis plainly. Name the misconception, do not scold. Show the number when there is one. No hype, no exclamation, no em-dash. The student did the work; the screen reflects it back.
