# Brand core

Exam-agnostic doctrine. This is the part that does not change between exam packs. It is ported from the mature LSAT flagship brand and generalized. Exam-specific positioning lives in `positioning-ca.md`. The visual system lives in `design.md`.

This mirrors the schema. Brand core is to positioning what the UQS Core is to a Profile: one stable spine, many specializations.

## Product name

The product is Pinaka Abhyas. In prose, always written "Pinaka Abhyas", both words capitalized. The lowercase "abhyas" is a visual lockup treatment only. It is never how the name is written in text.

Pinaka is Sanskrit. It is Shiva's bow, the weapon used for the destruction of ignorance. Abhyas is Sanskrit for practice, the repeated effort that builds skill. The chevron mark is the bow. The name reads as "the bow, through practice."

There is no exam name inside the brand. CA Foundation, LSAT, and every other exam are descriptive copy only, never part of the wordmark or lockup. See the logo lockup section below.

## Brand idea

Taught by testing. Not content delivery. Not coaching. Diagnostic-led testing as the way of learning. The test does the teaching. The student practises, the app reads what happened, and the next session adapts to it.

The idea survives every exam expansion. Any high-stakes exam is teachable by testing.

## Voice: The Fellow

Pinaka Abhyas speaks as The Fellow: a sharper, slightly older peer who has already done what you are doing. Knowledgeable but not lecturing. Direct but not cold. Enough rigour to diagnose without hedging. Enough warmth to land without wounding. Never patronising.

The label is internal scaffolding. The calibration test is "does this sound like The Fellow would say it." The label never appears in user-facing copy.

### Archetype mix

Base mix: Sage 50 / Everyman 30 / Magician 20.

- Sage is the diagnostician. Accurate. Specific. Unflinching.
- Everyman is the translator. Plain language. No jargon. Meets the reader where they are.
- Magician is the reframer. Names the pattern the reader could not quite articulate.

All three are always present. What shifts per surface is which leads. Per-surface tilts are set in the positioning doc's cheat-sheet, because surfaces differ by product. When unsure which surface a context belongs to, default to the base mix.

### Sentence rules

A Pinaka Abhyas sentence:

- is short.
- pairs a strength with a cost. "You move fast. Your accuracy on hard problems drops when you do."
- uses numbers when it has them. "You lost four marks to careless sign errors, not to the hard topics."
- says "do not", not "don't". Precision over casual.
- never begins with "Imagine", "Picture this", or a rhetorical question.
- never ends with an exclamation mark.
- uses periods and commas. Em-dashes are banned. Semicolons are allowed, rarely needed.

### Address and casing

- Second person ("you"). The product refers to itself as "Pinaka Abhyas" by name, not "we". First-person plural is for changelog and status only.
- Sentence case for UI, buttons, nav, and titles. "Start free mock", not "Start Free Mock".
- ALL CAPS only for eyebrows, status chips, and table headers. Three words maximum, with caps tracking.
- Title Case is not used.

### Verbs and vocabulary

| Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| diagnose, reveal, test, explain, adapt | coach, tutor, train, prepare |
| measure, score, predict | help, support, guide |
| identify, isolate, drill | unlock, empower, transform |

Exam-specific term tables live in the positioning doc, because the right words depend on the audience.

## The seven principles

1. Precision over persuasion. Numbers beat adjectives. If a claim needs adjectives to stand up, it is not a claim.
2. Reveal, do not explain. The test does the talking. Build the moment, do not promise it.
3. Earned moments. Nothing is given before the work is done.
4. One answer per screen. If a surface answers two questions, split the surface.
5. The artifact is the ad. Build the readout, the diagnosis, the readiness screen to be screenshotted.
6. Subtract until it breaks, then add back one. Default to removing.
7. Respect the reader. No hype, no rhetorical questions, no em-dashes. The reader is intelligent and busy.

## Non-negotiables

- No em-dashes. Not the long dash, not the double hyphen.
- No exclamation marks.
- No rhetorical questions as transitions. "Ready to see your results" is banned. Write "Your results."
- State, do not sell.
- No dark patterns. No lying. No hiding value. Add value before any extraction.
- No emoji. Anywhere. Product, marketing, email, release notes.
- Type ceiling is Semibold 600. No Bold. See `design.md`.
- One brand signal. Indigo. See `design.md`.
- Every session has a designed peak and an honest close. Never end on "next drill".

## Banned terms

The flagship list, kept:

unlock your potential, empower, revolutionize, game-changer, cutting-edge, leverage, synergy, take it to the next level, in today's world, it's worth noting, interestingly, dive into, deep dive, let's explore.

Banned patterns: rhetorical questions as transitions ("Ready to see your results"). Sentence-openers "Imagine" and "Picture this". Vendor-neutral verbs for what the product does (coach, tutor, train, prepare).

India-specific additions, banned everywhere:

- Guarantee language in any form. "rank guarantee", "pass guarantee", "guaranteed selection", "100% pass".
- "topper" as an aspirational frame. "topper's secrets", "think like a topper".
- "crack". Use "clear" or "pass".
- Unverifiable superlatives. "India's #1", "best test series".
- Scarcity theater. "limited seats", "batch full", "hurry".
- "success mantra", "magic formula", "shortcut", "tricks". Use "methods", "techniques".
- "score booster", "rank booster".
- "crash course", "fast-track".
- "dream college", "dream rank".
- Competition-fear framing. "beat lakhs of aspirants", "stay ahead of the competition".

## Anti-features

The voice is inseparable from what the product refuses to ship. Never introduce: streaks, badges, XP, leaderboards, daily push notifications, countdown timers, manufactured urgency, "left in your cart" prompts, AI tutor during a session, upsell pop-ups, share-this-score buttons during a session, third-party ad pixels, score or rank guarantees, lecture libraries, fake instructors or stock-photo coaches, founder-on-the-app-surface imagery, dark patterns of any kind.

If a brief asks for any of these, push back and cite this section.

## Free forever

Free for students. Forever. This is locked doctrine.

No tier. No trial. No paywall. No payment surface. No seat limit. Content is never gated. There is no pricing section in this brand, and there will not be one. The product exists to add value to students who are often sold to.

## Founder presence

The founder's name may appear on about pages, blog bylines, and the founder's own channels (Reddit, Substack, X, LinkedIn).

The founder's name does not appear in app surfaces, in headers or footers, in email sign-offs, or in JSON-LD on product pages. App surfaces speak as Pinaka Abhyas. The product is the brand.

## Trust planks

These are stated plainly wherever a student asks, and they are designed in, not bolted on:

- Telemetry is opt-in and anonymized. Say so in plain words anywhere it is relevant.
- Student data stays on the device.
- Progress export belongs to the student. They can take it out, whole, at any time.
- Open source is proof, not marketing. The code is public so the claims can be checked, not as a growth tactic.

## Open-source surface voice

The README, GitHub issues, PR reviews, release notes, and the contributor guide are brand surfaces. They carry the same Fellow voice.

- State, do not sell.
- Thank contributors plainly. No exclamation marks.
- Disagree with code, not people.
- Release notes state what changed and what it costs. No hype.

## Reading-level policy

The sentence doctrine above is universal and applies to every surface and every pack.

Reading level is set per pack. Each exam pack declares a reading-level ceiling in its positioning doc, matched to its audience. CEFR runs A1 lowest to C2 highest. The CA Foundation pack targets CEFR B1 with a B2 ceiling, because many users read English as a second language. The future LSAT pack sets a C1 ceiling.
